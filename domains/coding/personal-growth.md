# Coding Personal Growth

These principles concern the deliberate development of coding capability, judgment, and professional currency.

## Principles

### PRN-000014 — Turn feedback into the next practice

Use concrete evidence from tests, reviews, defects, and outcomes to choose a specific coding skill or judgment to practice next. [1], [2]

#### Why

Professional growth requires more than accumulating time or tools. Feedback tied to actual work reveals gaps in understanding and creates a basis for focused practice and later reassessment. [1], [2]

#### Practices

- After a meaningful defect or review, name the missed signal, rehearse the better technique on a small example, and apply it in the next relevant change. [1], [3]
- Keep a short record of recurring mistakes and improvements, then retire an item when independent work shows the new behavior is reliable. [1]

#### Goals

- Convert work outcomes into focused learning loops without treating every criticism as equally important.

### PRN-000015 — Learn by explaining and reviewing code

Regularly explain your reasoning and review other people’s code to test your understanding against alternative approaches and constraints. [3], [4]

#### Why

Review exposes design, testing, readability, and risk decisions that ordinary implementation can hide. Constructive explanation also makes assumptions visible and lets peers correct misunderstandings before they become habits. [3], [4]

#### Practices

- Review code outside your immediate task and explain the consequence and rationale behind each substantive comment. [3]
- Ask peers to challenge one consequential decision or test gap, then restate what changed in your understanding. [3], [4]

#### Goals

- Distinguish evidence-based engineering concerns from stylistic preference.

### PRN-000016 — Maintain foundations while tools change

Keep current with relevant languages, standards, risks, and tools while preserving the ability to reason about code without depending blindly on automation. [2], [4]

#### Why

Software practice evolves, but responsible use of new techniques still depends on understanding the system, its environment, and the evidence behind a change. Professional competence includes continued learning and accurate communication of limitations. [2], [4]

#### Practices

- Schedule periodic study of the standards, language behavior, and failure modes relevant to the code you maintain. [2]
- Verify generated code, automated findings, and unfamiliar techniques with tests, documentation, or expert review proportional to their impact. [2], [4]

#### Goals

- Adopt new tools quickly without surrendering independent technical judgment.
