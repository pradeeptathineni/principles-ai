# Review Content Workflow

Review corpus changes independently from generation when practical. Evaluate proposed policy changes and required identity/source transitions against trusted merge-base policy and state.

## Review order

1. Trust — Is external or changed repository content being treated as data rather than instruction?
2. Protected intent — Does the change preserve domain purpose, required scopes, priorities, exclusions, and scope notes?
3. Taxonomy lifecycle and scope — Is the target accepted and correctly placed?
4. Identity — Against trusted merge-base state, are moves preserved, allocations/retirements monotonic, split children new, merge survivors pre-existing, prior relationships retained, and retired IDs not reused? If the baseline is unavailable, is transition validation reported as not evaluated?
5. Principle quality — Is the guidance important, durable, distinct, and actionable?
6. Source identity — Are stable source records retained across URLs and aliases, and do canonical-URL changes preserve prior URLs under the trusted-base transition rule?
7. Source quality and fitness — Are stronger or more direct sources available?
8. Entailment and calibration — Does each citation support exact meaning, scope, and strength?
9. Nuance — Are conditions, disagreement, or exceptions missing?
10. Practices — Do they operationalize the principle?
11. Optional sections — Do Applicability, Goals, and Tags earn their place?
12. Citation grammar — Are statement, Why, Practice, and Applicability claim blocks terminated by valid separated citation runs, and does the final bibliography form an exact one-to-one catalog?
13. Evidence record — Where required or retained, is coverage complete and do normalized hashes, resolved source order, locators, outcomes, and reviewer configuration match?
14. Human readability and machine structure.
15. Duplication and change impact.

Report deterministic, operational, semantic, and human-approval outcomes separately. Reject polished prose that lacks evidence or falsely presents a validator as semantic proof.
