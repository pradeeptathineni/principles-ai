# AI Context Architecture v1 Readiness Review — 2026-09-02

## Scope and authority

This readiness review used the current working tree as the system of record and `HEAD` only as trusted-baseline support for transition classification. It evaluated the router, architecture, routinely routed workflows, relevant semantic standards, templates, schema/version guidance, and deterministic routing/workset support. Historical reviews remain noncanonical; current behavior is defined by the revised canonical files.

## Implemented findings

- Reduced `AGENTS.md` to routing, universal invariants, authority, and gates.
- Made domain furnishing/refinement an orchestration-only entry route. Content, update, and taxonomy guidance loads only at the stage that delegates to it.
- Kept routine content creation self-contained for the critical research/selection/citation/qualification/activation path while moving exact and disputed cases to canonical standards.
- Consolidated taxonomy lifecycle/operation semantics in the Taxonomy standard and shortened the update workflow to state inspection, research, operation choice, validation, decision, and resumption.
- Removed exact executable citation, source-normalization, and claim-hash procedures from duplicated prose while preserving semantic entailment, fitness, identity, lifecycle, and invalidation rules.
- Reorganized architecture around authority, canonical/derived state, semantic boundaries, routing/state composition, deterministic mutation, validation classes, and trust.
- Split the live schema/version contract from retained migration history. Routine schema/policy work no longer loads completed migration narratives; the history remains linked and available when relevant.
- Replaced the completed phased bootstrap narrative with the maintained harness boundary and capability checklist.
- Simplified templates to the minimum grammar and explicitly denied preferred counts or shapes; removed optional-section, second-practice, second-source, and multi-citation examples that could become learned defaults.

## Semantic sufficiency and intentional recurrence

The following remain repeated in compact local form because omission or a forced large-file lookup would cost more or increase risk:

- research and source reading before synthesis;
- broad candidate discovery, coverage analysis, comparison/consolidation, and subject-determined selection;
- citation presence versus entailment and source fitness;
- meaningful contextual recurrence versus harmful duplication;
- applicability, conditions, exceptions, disagreement, and protected intent;
- canonical versus derived state and stable identity;
- deterministic, operational, semantic, and human authority separation;
- untrusted-content and trusted-baseline limits;
- exact human gates and automatic workflow resumption.

The repeated reminders are short; detailed definitions have one canonical semantic owner.
